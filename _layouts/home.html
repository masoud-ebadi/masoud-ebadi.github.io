Go Beyond Mapping. Understand Cities Through Spatial Analysis.

Urban planning requires more than visualizing locations on a map. It involves understanding spatial patterns, measuring accessibility, evaluating urban conditions, identifying suitable locations, and analyzing how cities change over time.

This course introduces practical GIS techniques using ArcGIS Pro to help learners investigate urban challenges and develop stronger spatial analysis skills.

Practical Applications

These methods are ways to examine a city with spatial evidence. They support study and decision-making. They do not replace local knowledge, field checks, or the limits of the data.

  • Urban planning studies

    Compare form, density, land use, and change across parcels, neighborhoods, and districts.

  • Spatial decision-making

    Set criteria, compare locations, and see how a proposed pattern relates to existing conditions.

  • Site selection

    Test whether candidate sites meet spatial conditions such as access, compatibility, and vacancy.

  • Public service planning

    Estimate where facilities could cover more people, and where coverage is thin.

  • Accessibility assessment

    Measure reach along the street network, not only distance across the map. See the guide to urban accessibility analysis using GIS.

  • Urban growth monitoring

    Follow building, block, and population change over time instead of relying on a single snapshot.

  • Spatial pattern detection

    Ask whether incidents, density, or land-use change are clustered, dispersed, or arranged along a direction. Related reading: population density analysis in ArcGIS Pro.
